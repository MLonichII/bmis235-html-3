F80 3

The fifth-generation M3 introduced a new direction for BMW's performance lineup. Released in 2014, the F80 M3 marked the return to a six-cylinder engine after the V8-powered E9X generation. However, this time BMW introduced twin-turbocharging, dramatically changing how the M3 delivered its power. Another major change was the separation of the coupe and sedan models: the four-door sedan retained the M3 name, while the coupe became the BMW M4. The F80 featured sharper styling, a wider stance, more aggressive bodywork, and extensive lightweight construction, including a carbon-fiber roof and carbon-fiber driveshaft. These changes helped create a more modern performance sedan while maintaining the M3's recognizable motorsport character. The F80 was known for its impressive acceleration, strong midrange power, and advanced driving technology. Its twin-turbocharged engine produced significantly more torque than the naturally aspirated engines of previous generations, making it exceptionally quick in everyday driving conditions. BMW also introduced several versions throughout its production, including the Competition Package and the limited-production M3 CS. While some enthusiasts preferred the sound and high-revving nature of earlier naturally aspirated M3s, the F80 demonstrated how turbocharging could dramatically improve performance without abandoning the traditional rear-wheel-drive M3 formula. It became an important transition point between the older, naturally aspirated M cars and the increasingly powerful modern generation.

The F80 M3 was powered by the S55, a 3.0-liter twin-turbocharged inline-six producing 425 horsepower in its original North American specification. The Competition Package increased output to 444 horsepower, while the later M3 CS produced 453 horsepower. The S55 delivered substantially more torque at lower engine speeds than the naturally aspirated engines before it, making the F80 particularly strong during acceleration. Its combination of turbocharging, direct injection, and advanced engine management helped establish the foundation for BMW's modern turbocharged M engines.
