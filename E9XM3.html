E9X M3

The fourth-generation M3 marked one of the biggest mechanical changes in the model's history. Introduced in 2007, the E9X generation replaced the traditional inline-six with a naturally aspirated 4.0-liter V8 engine. This was the first and, to date, only production M3 generation to use a V8. The car was available in three different body styles: the E92 coupe, E90 sedan, and E93 convertible. Its styling was more muscular than the E46, featuring a sculpted hood, wider bodywork, aggressive front and rear bumpers, and a distinctive power dome on the hood. The E92 coupe became particularly recognizable for its combination of modern styling and naturally aspirated V8 performance. The E9X M3 was designed to deliver a more powerful and high-performance driving experience without losing the everyday practicality that had become part of the M3 identity. Its V8 engine could rev beyond 8,000 rpm, giving it a sound and personality unlike any other generation. BMW also introduced a seven-speed M-DCT dual-clutch transmission alongside the traditional six-speed manual, improving gear changes and performance. Special versions such as the M3 GTS and M3 CRT pushed the platform even further through increased power and weight reduction. The E9X generation remains especially important because it represented a brief period when BMW placed a high-revving naturally aspirated V8 into its compact performance sedan and coupe..

The E9X M3 used the S65, a 4.0-liter naturally aspirated V8 producing 414 horsepower in North American models. The engine was closely related in design philosophy to the V10 used in the E60 M5 and featured an exceptionally high 8,400-rpm redline. Unlike turbocharged engines that produce substantial torque at lower engine speeds, the S65 rewarded drivers who allowed it to rev, delivering its power with a distinctive high-pitched exhaust note. It remains one of the most recognizable engines in BMW M history and is a major reason the E9X M3 has such a devoted following.
