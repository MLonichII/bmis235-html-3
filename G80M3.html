G80 M3

The sixth-generation M3 represents the most technologically advanced and powerful evolution of the model to date. Introduced in 2020, the G80 brought a dramatic redesign, including the large vertical kidney grille that became one of the most recognizable and debated features of the modern BMW lineup. Beneath its updated exterior, the G80 received a completely new chassis, revised suspension, improved cooling systems, and a more advanced interior. BMW continued offering a six-speed manual transmission in rear-wheel-drive models, while Competition versions introduced an eight-speed automatic transmission. For the first time in M3 history, BMW also made its M xDrive all-wheel-drive system available, giving drivers the option of increased traction and exceptionally quick acceleration. The G80 continued expanding the M3 lineup with several performance-focused versions, including the Competition, Competition xDrive, M3 CS, and the first production M3 Touring. The Touring brought the M3's performance to a wagon body style, combining serious performance with additional practicality. The G80 also introduced a new level of technology, including advanced driver assistance systems, digital displays, configurable driving modes, and sophisticated electronic chassis controls. Although its styling and increased size have divided enthusiasts, the G80 has continued the M3 tradition of combining everyday usability with genuine motorsport-inspired performance.

The G80 M3 uses the S58, a 3.0-liter twin-turbocharged inline-six developed specifically for BMW's modern M performance lineup. Power output varies by version, beginning at approximately 473 horsepower in the standard North American M3 and reaching 523 horsepower in the M3 CS. Competition models also offer substantially increased torque and, in xDrive form, exceptional acceleration. The S58 features advanced turbocharging, direct injection, and sophisticated cooling technology designed to support sustained high-performance driving. It represents the latest stage in the evolution of BMW's inline-six engines, bringing together the high-revving character of earlier M engines with the power and efficiency of modern turbocharging.
