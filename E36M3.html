E36 M3

The second-generation M3 represented a major change in BMW's approach to its performance lineup. Introduced in 1992, the E36 M3 moved away from the original car's racing-focused design and became a more refined, comfortable, and powerful sports car. Its styling was more rounded and understated than the E30, reflecting the design direction of the 1990s. It was available as a coupe, sedan, and convertible, making the M3 accessible to a wider range of drivers. While it was less visually aggressive than its predecessor, the E36 brought significant improvements in chassis development, suspension, braking, and everyday drivability. One of the most important changes was the introduction of a six-cylinder engine, giving the M3 a smoother and more powerful character. European models initially received a 3.0-liter engine, later upgraded to a 3.2-liter version. North American models received different engine specifications, with the 1995 M3 using the S50B30US and later models using the S52. The E36 helped transform the M3 from a specialized homologation car into a more widely recognized luxury performance vehicle. It also helped establish the six-cylinder engine as an important part of the M3 identity.

The European E36 M3 used the S50 inline-six, producing approximately 286 horsepower in its original 3.0-liter form and up to 321 horsepower in the later 3.2-liter version. North American versions used the S50B30US and S52 engines, producing approximately 240 horsepower. Compared with the original S14 four-cylinder, the inline-six delivered more torque, smoother power delivery, and a broader performance range. It marked the beginning of the M3's long relationship with high-revving six-cylinder engines.
